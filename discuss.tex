\subsection*{The four groups of SOD2 proteins form distinct clusters in an
unrooted phylogenetic tree, and subclusters within each coral protein group
correlate with genus}

160 mature SOD2 sequences consisting of the 123 from coral and symbiodinium,
plus 37 from annotated reference SOD2 proteins from across the tree of life,
were clustered (98\% sequence similarity) using MMSeqs (\cite{mmseqs2}),
aligned using MUSCLE, and organized into an unrooted phylogenetic tree using
IQTree (\cite{iqtree}). Figure~\ref{fig:tree} shows the tree as visualized
using iTOL (\cite{itol}).

Each of the four query groups (SP_cnidaria, SP_cTP_algae, SP_algae,
mT_cnidaria) forms an exclusive clade sharing a unique common ancestor that
includes every member of that specific set. This confirms that each targeting
subgroup represents a coherent gene family that evolved together without
intermixing with the others, that the mature domain co-evolved in lockstep with
the target signals, and that the four groups likely evolved completely
independently from earlier gene duplication events.

mT_cnidaria is a canonical mitochondrial MnSOD, clustered with reference
primate/fungal sequences, with the enclosing fungal node at 100. mT_cnidaria
cluster well with other primate mitochondrial SOD, and evolved through the
mitochondrial acquisition/endosymbiosis. sp_cnidaria evolved distantly from
mt_cnidaria, and independent of the mitochondrial evolution/adaption.

sp_algae and sp_ctp_algae are paralogs that may have came from duplication
events. They are distinct groups. ctp is closer to Phadactylum which is a
diatom, red secondary plastid, than to sp. SP_cTP_algae is specifically sister
to Phaeodactylum tricornutum FeSOD at 100 — the strongest query-to-reference
relationship present.


\subsection*{Unverified metal specificity}

Metal specificity is a spectrum, not a binary: there are Mn-specific,
Fe-specific, and cambialistic SODs that work with either ion, and the modern
distribution is polyphyletic. Specificity is tuned by the redox potential of
the active-site metal, and several non-conserved second-sphere residues
contribute to that tuning; consequently prediction methods based solely on
primary sequence have not given strong predictive values. There are documented
sequence/metal mismatches — e.g. the Sinorhizobium meliloti SOD has an
iron-type sequence but preferentially incorporates Mn in vivo. 

mT_cnidaria is a canonical mitochondrial MnSOD. SP_cTP_algae is specifically
sister to Phaeodactylum tricornutum FeSOD at 100 — the strongest
query-to-reference relationship present.

Tree position does not determine cofactor. E.g. Fragilariopsis FeSOD pairs with
Phaeodactylum MnSOD at UFBoot 97.

\subsection*{Disagreement with prior grouping}

Krueger et al.

Discusses Mt/S and Chloroplast versions in algae, but they took annotations
from published sources and presented alignments assuming those annotations were
correct. They noticed bigger differences between their MnSOD3 group with other
groups. This is because their MnSOD3 is in fact a SP targeting one, while their
MnSOD1 and MnSOD2 groups are actually our sp\_cpt groups. E.g. several SymMnSOD1
and SymMnSOD2 scored above 442 for SP\_cTP\_algae, while two SymMnSOD3
sequences scored above 400 for SP\_algae.
