
\section*{Results}

Manual inspection of DeepLoc 2.1 results over full length SOD2 sequences
suggests the presence of three patterns: a) a mitochondrial transit peptide
(mTP) between 15 to 35 AAs upstream of PF00081 with sequence location predicted
to be mitochondria, b) a signal peptide (SP) between 15 to 40 AAs upstream of
PF00081 with sequence location predicted to be extracellular, and c) a signal
peptide between 50 and 70 AAs upstream of PF00081 with sequence location
predicted to be the golgi apparatus. Specifically, the first and last
predictions were almost exclusively for coral hosts and symbiodiniums,
respectively, while the middle prediction includes sequences for both hosts and
symbionts.

Additional inspection using DeepLoc, on the SP and golgi apparatus sequences,
reveal that once the SP is cleaved, DeepLoc predicts the remaining sequence as
plastid bound. This observation is consistent with general consensus of
bipartite leader architecture for chloroplast targeting proteins in cells that
have gone through secondary endosymbiosis \cite{mcfadden1999plantbio}.

Based on these observations, leader discovery algorithm was adjusted to
specifically look for start sites in these ranges, and the full length SOD2
sequences were separated into four groups: mTP in coral, SP in coral,
SP+golgi in symbiodinium, and SP in symbiodinium. Multi-sequence alignments of
mature protein sequences within each bucket showed high degree of conservation.
Overall, 147 full length SOD2 sequences, 123 unique, exhibited these DeepLoc
called features. Table~\ref{tab:class_breakdown} shows the breakdown by group.
Figure~\ref{fig:taxa} shows breakdown by taxonomy -- the first columns on the
left are transcriptomic sequences, then the rest of the columns are grouped by
taxonomy of the genomic sequences.

\subsection*{Four groups of SOD2 sequences, two in coral and two in
symbiodinium, are clearly distinguishable from each other, and covers >90\% of
sequences with SOD2 signature}

\begin{figure}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{hmm_scores.png}
  \caption{\texttt{hmmsearch} scores of protein groups (each in a pane), against all the HMM profiles (columns).}
\label{fig:hmmscores}
\end{figure}

For each protein group, a HMM profile of the mature protein sequences was
constructed using hmmer3 (\cite{eddy2011hmmer}). Figure~\ref{fig:hmmscores}
shows that the four HMM profiles are clearly distinguishable; when scoring each
group against all four profiles, the correct profile, in orange, scores the
highest, with no overlap with other scores. The lowest score from each set of
orange marks is used as the "threshold score" for the corresponding HMM
profile. The values are 303.5, 262.4, 441.8, and 421.3, respectively.

To test if there are other undiscovered SOD2 protein groups, 114 unique mature
protein sequences were selected from all the genomic and RNAseq sequences that
matched to the K04564 HMM profile, without checking for plausible start codons,
types of leader sequences, and targeted cellular compartment. These 114
sequences were then scored against the HMM profiles from the four groups. All
but 12 sequences scored above the threshold for a HMM profile. Out of the 12
negatives, 4 are just below the threshold for SP\_cTP\_algae and can be
considered FNs. The remaining 8, or 7\% are unknown sequences. Thus, the four
HMM profiles are able to cover 93\% of all K04564 matching sequences in this
paper's datasets.

Interestingly, SP\_cnidaria sequences are missing from all the genomes under
the \textit{Acroporidae} family. The \textit{Acropora tenuis} RNAseq data from
\cite{pm32} was captured against its genomic sequences, which may also explain
the lack of a SP\_cnidaria sequence in that transcriptome. It is unclear if
this omission in the genome is real, or due to some genome assembly artifact.

\subsection*{The four groups of SOD2 proteins form distinct clades in an
unrooted phylogenetic tree, suggesting independent evolution}

\begin{figure}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{tree_full.png}
  \caption{Sample figure caption.}
  \label{fig:tree}
\end{figure}

160 mature SOD2 sequences consisting of the 123 from coral and symbiodinium,
plus 37 from annotated reference SOD2 proteins from across the tree of life,
were clustered (98\% sequence similarity) using MMSeqs (\cite{mmseqs2}),
aligned using MUSCLE, and organized into an unrooted phylogenetic tree using
IQTree (\cite{iqtree}).

Figure~\ref{fig:tree} shows the tree as visualized using iTOL (\cite{itol}).
Each of the four protein groups forms an exclusive clade that includes every
member of the group and shares a unique common ancestor. This tree structure
suggests these four groups evovled independently, without intermixing with each
other. Additionally, the mature protein sequences co-evolved, in lockstep, with
the localization signals.
